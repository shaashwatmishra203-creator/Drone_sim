# 08 — Factory Navigation and Mapping Mission

> **Verification level: L0–L2.** Simulation only. Masses still estimated.

Launch from base, navigate a slalom obstacle course through a factory-like
environment, scan a room at the far end, return to base and land.

---

## The environment

`worlds/factory.sdf`, generated by `tools/gen_world.py` so the layout lives in
version control rather than a hand-edited XML file.

```
        y=+6 ____________________________________
             |     ###     |     ###      |      |        +-------------+
             |     ###  G1 |     ###  G3  |      |        |             |
        y=0  |  O  ...  ...  ...  ...  ...  ...  | ====>  |    ROOM     |
             | base    ### |          ### |      |        |  12 x 12 m  |
             |   G0    ### |    G2    ### |      |        +-------------+
        y=-6 |____________________________________|
             x=3   8    12    16    20   24        26      32        38

        G0..G3 = gates: full-height barriers across the corridor,
                 each with ONE 2.5 m gap, alternating side to side.
```

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Launch base | 2.4 × 2.4 m pad at the origin. **Visual only, no collision body** |
| Corridor | Walled, y = ±6 m, from x = 3 to 24 m, 4 m tall |
| Gates | 4, at x = 8/12/16/20 m, **2.5 m gap** alternating at y = ±2.4 m, 4 m tall |
| Room | 12 × 12 m, 4 m tall, four walls with a 4 m doorway on the −x face |
| Room contents | Two crates (1.5 m, 2.0 m) and a machine (2.2 m) |

The gates are **full height**, so there is no flying over them, and the
corridor is walled, so there is no going around. The only way through is the
gap. Against a 0.5 m airframe, a 2.5 m gap leaves about 1 m either side.

## The mission profile

`scenarios/factory_mission.yaml`, flown by the `factory_mission` profile in
`mission_runner`. Four legs, timed separately:

1. **Outbound** — 11 waypoints threading the four gates, with a line-up point
   before each so the aircraft arrives square rather than diagonally. It yaws
   to face its direction of travel so the cameras look where it is going.
2. **Scan** — an 8-point lawnmower sweep at **3.0 m** with a continuous
   0.35 rad/s yaw sweep, covering walls, floor and objects.
3. **Inbound** — back through all four gates to base.
4. **Land** — descend onto the pad.

Light wind (1.5 m/s, 0.8 m/s gusts) is applied after takeoff.

> The scan leg measures what a mapping pass **costs** in time and energy. It
> does not build a map — that needs the ZED SDK, which requires CUDA and does
> not run in SITL. See [09-compute-architecture.md](09-compute-architecture.md).

## Two defects this course exposed

Both were found by measurement after the flight *looked* fine, which is the
point of having `check_clearance.py` at all.

### 1. The first course did not constrain the flight path

The original layout put storage racks off to the sides of the route and thin
pillars on the centreline. Watching it, the aircraft appeared to weave through
an obstacle course. Measured, it did not: nothing came within a metre of the
hull, and the path **passed straight through three pillars** — closest approach
0.00 m, meaning the trajectory went inside the solid geometry.

Two separate problems:

- The racks were never near the route. A drone with no perception at all would
  have flown that course successfully. It tested waypoint following.
- A 1.2 m acceptance radius let the controller cut corners badly enough to clip
  the centreline pillars.

Fixed by replacing scenery with **gates** — full-height barriers with a single
gap, in a walled corridor, so there is no path except through — and tightening
the acceptance radius to 0.7 m.

### 2. Declaring one plugin in a world silently removes all the others

While fixing the course, the world briefly declared only the `WindEffects`
plugin, on the reasoning that PX4's stock worlds declare none and therefore
inherit defaults.

**In Gazebo, declaring any `<plugin>` in a world replaces the default system
set rather than adding to it.** Declaring one removed Physics, SceneBroadcaster
and Sensors. The world still loaded and still published
`/world/factory/clock`, but the `/world/factory/scene/info` service never
appeared — and that service is what PX4's `px4-rc.gzsim` actually waits on. PX4
printed `Waiting for Gazebo world...` thirty times and exited. No error message
anywhere pointed at the cause.

**If a world declares plugins at all, it must declare them all.** `factory.sdf`
declares plugins only because it needs `WindEffects`, which PX4's stock worlds
do not include.

### Also: the launch pad must be visual-only

An early version gave the base pad a collision body. The vehicle spawned
against it and spent **88 seconds at 2.4 kW fighting the ground** before
breaking free, burning 34 Wh and wrecking the energy accounting. A launch base
is a painted floor marking; modelling it as one removed the problem.

## What this does and does not establish

**Does:** the aircraft can fly a genuinely constrained course — four 2.5 m
gates with no way over or around — hold a scan pattern, and return to base with
very large energy reserve, on the current sub-optimal 9-inch propellers.

**Does not:**

- **No obstacle avoidance was tested.** The waypoints are pre-planned and now
  verified collision-free. The aircraft flew a known-good path; it did not
  perceive the gates and decide how to pass them. Closing that gap needs the
  depth pipeline, which needs CUDA hardware — see
  [09-compute-architecture.md](09-compute-architecture.md).
- **No map was produced.** The scan leg measures the cost of the motion, not
  the quality of the result.
- **Speed is conservative** at 1.50 m/s mean, chosen as a survey speed.

```

python3 tools/check_clearance.py && python3 tools/analyze_mission.py
```
